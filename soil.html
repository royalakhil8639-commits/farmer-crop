Soil Management

Learn the major soil types of India, which crops they suit, how much water they hold — and how to keep soil healthy for decades of productive farming.

Understanding Soil

What is Soil?

Soil is a living, layered mixture of minerals, organic matter, water, air and countless organisms. It anchors roots, stores water and supplies nutrients to crops.

Soil forms over thousands of years from weathered rock and decomposed plants and animals. Its upper layer — topsoil — holds most organic matter and biological activity, which is why protecting it from erosion is critical.

  • Minerals (~45%) — sand, silt and clay particles from rock
  • Organic matter (~5%) — decomposed plant and animal material
  • Water (~25%) — stored in pore spaces for roots
  • Air (~25%) — oxygen for roots and soil organisms

Across India

Major Soil Types

India has diverse soils. Matching the crop to your soil type is the first step to a good harvest.

Alluvial Soil

Characteristics: Fertile, deposited by rivers; found in the Indo-Gangetic plains and deltas.

Suitable crops: Rice, wheat, sugarcane, maize, pulses, oilseeds.

Water holding: Moderate to high.

Importance: India’s most productive agricultural zone.

Black Soil (Regur)

Characteristics: Clayey “cotton soil”, cracks in summer, self-mulching; Deccan plateau.

Suitable crops: Cotton, soybean, sorghum, sunflower, pulses.

Water holding: Very high.

Importance: Backbone of rainfed cotton and oilseed farming.

Red Soil

Characteristics: Red from iron oxide; low in nitrogen and organic matter; eastern and southern plateaus.

Suitable crops: Groundnut, millets, pulses, potato, tobacco.

Water holding: Low to moderate.

Importance: Responds very well to organic manure and irrigation.

Laterite Soil

Characteristics: Leached, acidic soil of high-rainfall regions (Western Ghats, North-East).

Suitable crops: Cashew, rubber, tea, coffee, coconut, arecanut.

Water holding: Low; drains fast despite heavy rain.

Importance: Supports plantation economy crops.

Sandy Soil

Characteristics: Coarse particles, poor fertility, arid and desert regions (Rajasthan).

Suitable crops: Bajra (pearl millet), groundnut, watermelon, cluster bean.

Water holding: Very low.

Importance: Dryland farming and drought-hardy crops.

Clay Soil

Characteristics: Fine particles, sticky when wet, slow drainage.

Suitable crops: Paddy rice, wheat, lentils, broccoli.

Water holding: Very high.

Importance: Holds nutrients well; needs careful tillage timing.

Loamy Soil

Characteristics: Balanced mix of sand, silt and clay with good humus — the ideal garden and farm soil.

Suitable crops: Almost all crops — vegetables, wheat, sugarcane, fruits.

Water holding: Balanced; drains yet retains moisture.

Importance: The benchmark soil for intensive cultivation.

Living Soil

Soil Health Essentials

Healthy soil is fertile soil. Understand what to measure and how to improve it.

Soil pH

Measures acidity or alkalinity (ideal for most crops: 6.0–7.5). Lime corrects acidic soils; gypsum is used for sodic/alkaline soils as advised.

Organic Matter

The engine of soil life. It improves structure, water holding, nutrient supply and microbial activity. Build it with compost and residues.

Nitrogen (N)

Drives leaf and vegetative growth. Deficiency shows as pale, yellowing older leaves; excess makes crops weak and late.

Phosphorus (P)

Essential for roots, flowering and early vigour. Deficiency causes stunted, purplish plants and poor establishment.

Potassium (K)

Improves water regulation, disease resistance and quality of grain and fruit. Deficiency scorches leaf edges.

Micronutrients

Zinc, iron, boron, sulphur and others are needed in tiny amounts but strongly affect yield; diagnose with soil testing.

Soil Testing

Collect representative samples every 2–3 years through government soil health labs/KVKs and plan fertilizer from the report.

Compost

Decomposed farm waste and FYM return nutrients and carbon to soil, improving texture and reducing fertilizer need.

Green Manure

Fast-growing legumes (sunhemp, dhaincha) ploughed in while green add nitrogen and organic matter cheaply.

Crop Rotation

Alternating crop families — especially with legumes — balances nutrient use, breaks pest cycles and keeps soil alive.
