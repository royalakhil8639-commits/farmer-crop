Major Agricultural Seasons

Indian agriculture follows four broad cropping seasons. Actual sowing and harvesting periods vary by region and local conditions.

Kharif

Period: June–October

Weather: Monsoon, warm and humid

Crops: Rice, maize, cotton, sugarcane, soybean, groundnut, pigeonpea

Rabi

Period: October–March

Weather: Cool and dry winter

Crops: Wheat, potato, onion, chickpea, mustard, tomato

Zaid

Period: March–June

Weather: Hot summer, irrigated short season

Crops: Watermelon, cucumber, muskmelon, summer moong, fodder

Region-Specific

Period: Varies by state and agro-climatic zone

Weather: Depends on local rainfall and temperature

Crops: Millets in drylands, plantation and horticulture crops by region

Regional variationThe periods above are general. Always verify sowing and harvesting windows with your state agriculture department, as they shift with local weather and variety.

Practical Guidance

Farmer Knowledge Hub

Short, practical guidance on the everyday decisions that shape a successful crop season.

Weather Awareness

Follow local forecasts and agro-advisories. Plan sowing, spraying and harvesting around weather windows to reduce losses.

Seed Selection

Choose certified, region-appropriate varieties with resistance to local pests and diseases. Check germination before sowing.

Soil Testing

Test soil every 2–3 years to know pH, organic matter and nutrient levels — the basis for correct fertilizer planning.

Water Management

Irrigate at critical crop stages rather than on a fixed calendar. Drip and sprinkler systems can save 30–50% water.

Fertilizer Management

Apply nutrients based on soil test and crop stage. Split nitrogen, and combine fertilizers with organic matter.

Pest Monitoring

Walk fields weekly and scout for early pest signs. Acting early with correct thresholds saves both crop and money.

Crop Rotation

Rotate crops — especially with legumes — to break pest cycles, balance nutrients and improve long-term soil health.

Harvest Planning

Harvest at the right maturity stage. Arrange labour, machinery and drying space in advance to protect quality.

Storage

Dry produce to safe moisture before storage. Clean, aerated, pest-proof storage prevents major post-harvest losses.

Market Awareness

Track mandi prices and demand before selling. Diversifying produce and timing sales can significantly raise income.

Sustainable Agriculture

Farming That Protects the Future

Sustainable farming keeps soil fertile, water secure and farms profitable for the next generation. Key principles include:

  • Water conservation and rainwater harvesting
  • Soil conservation with cover crops and minimum disturbance
  • Crop diversification and rotation
  • Organic matter management — compost, green manure, residue recycling
  • Reduced unnecessary chemical use through IPM
  • Renewable energy such as solar pumps
  • Efficient irrigation — drip, sprinklers, scheduling
  • Biodiversity on and around the farm
