Water Management

Irrigation Methods

More crop per drop — compare how each irrigation method works, its advantages, limitations and the crops it suits best.

Animated water-flow visual — efficient irrigation moves water steadily to where roots need it.

01

Drip Irrigation

Water is delivered drop by drop directly to the root zone through pipes and emitters.

How It Works

  • Mainline, filters, laterals and emitters placed at each plant
  • Often combined with fertilizer injection (fertigation)

Advantages

  • Saves 30–60% water versus flood irrigation
  • Reduces weeds and leaf diseases
  • Higher yield and quality on the same water

Limitations

  • Higher initial cost
  • Emitters can clog — filtration and flushing needed

Suitable Crops

  • Vegetables, fruits, sugarcane, cotton, flowers, orchards
02

Sprinkler Irrigation

Water is sprayed over the crop like rainfall through nozzles and pipelines.

How It Works

  • Pump pressurises water through rotating sprinkler heads
  • Portable or permanent systems

Advantages

  • Works on uneven land without levelling
  • Saves water over surface flooding
  • Can also apply light fertilizer solutions

Limitations

  • Wind distorts spray pattern
  • Energy cost for pressure; leaf wetting can favour some diseases

Suitable Crops

  • Wheat, groundnut, vegetables, fodder, lawns and nurseries
03

Surface / Flood Irrigation

The traditional method — water flows by gravity across levelled fields.

How It Works

  • Channels release water onto flat plots
  • Soil absorbs water as the sheet advances

Advantages

  • No equipment cost; simple and widely available
  • Suits water-abundant canal and tank systems

Limitations

  • High water loss and uneven wetting
  • Can cause waterlogging and soil erosion on slopes

Suitable Crops

  • Paddy rice, wheat, fodder (with careful levelling and scheduling)
04

Furrow Irrigation

Water runs in small channels (furrows) between crop rows.

How It Works

  • Crops stand on ridges; water seeps sideways from furrows
  • Alternate or every furrow can be irrigated

Advantages

  • Uses less water than flooding the whole field
  • Cheap; suits row crops

Limitations

  • Tail-end furrows get less water
  • Labour for forming furrows; salts may accumulate on ridges

Suitable Crops

  • Cotton, maize, sugarcane, potato, vegetables
05

Basin Irrigation

Small level compartments with bunds are flooded and allowed to soak in.

How It Works

  • Level plots surrounded by bunds hold a shallow water sheet
  • Tree basins irrigate one plant at a time

Advantages

  • Uniform wetting when land is level
  • Simple for orchards and paddy

Limitations

  • Needs very level land
  • Water waste and stagnation risk if over-applied

Suitable Crops

  • Paddy rice, banana, orchards (tree basins)
06

Rainwater Harvesting

Collecting and storing rainfall in tanks, farm ponds and recharge structures for later use.

How It Works

  • Farm ponds, check dams and rooftop collection store runoff
  • Recharge pits revive groundwater

Advantages

  • Secures protective irrigation in dry spells
  • Recharges wells and reduces runoff loss

Limitations

  • Depends on rainfall amount and timing
  • Needs space and desilting maintenance

Suitable Crops

  • All crops benefit; lifeline for rainfed millets, pulses and vegetables

Golden ruleIrrigate at critical crop stages, water in cool hours, and match the method to your soil, slope and crop. Government schemes (like PMKSY micro-irrigation subsidy) can reduce drip and sprinkler costs — ask your district agriculture office.
