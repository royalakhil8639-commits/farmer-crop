Crop Protection

Pest & Disease Management

Identify common pests and diseases, understand how they damage crops, and learn Integrated Pest Management (IPM) — the safe, scientific way to protect harvests.

Know Your Enemy

Common Agricultural Pests

Correct identification is the first step of every control decision. These are the most frequent insect pests across Indian field and vegetable crops.

Aphids

Damage: Tiny green/black insects that suck sap from tender shoots, curl leaves and spread viruses.

Found on: Vegetables, mustard, pulses, cotton.

Early signs: Sticky honeydew, curled new leaves, ant activity.

Caterpillars (Borers)

Damage: Larvae of moths/butterflies that bore into stems, pods, fruits and bolls (e.g., pod borer, fruit borer).

Found on: Pigeonpea, chickpea, tomato, cotton, maize.

Early signs: Holes in fruits/pods, frass (insect droppings), wilting shoots.

Whiteflies

Damage: Small white flying insects under leaves; suck sap and transmit devastating viral diseases.

Found on: Cotton, tomato, chilli, soybean.

Early signs: White cloud when leaves are shaken, yellowing, honeydew with sooty mould.

Thrips

Damage: Minute slender insects that scrape leaf and flower surfaces causing silvery scars and distortions.

Found on: Onion, chilli, cotton, flowers.

Early signs: Silvery streaks, curled leaf margins, black specks of excreta.

Borers (Stem/Shoot)

Damage: Larvae tunnel inside stems and shoots — causing “dead hearts” in rice and “white ears”, or drying tops in brinjal and sugarcane.

Found on: Rice, sugarcane, brinjal, mango.

Early signs: Dead central shoot, bore holes with frass.

Grasshoppers & Locusts

Damage: Chew leaves, flowers and even young stems; migrating swarms can defoliate fields rapidly.

Found on: Millets, pulses, oilseeds, vegetables.

Early signs: Ragged leaf edges, heavy chewing damage from field borders inward.

Crop Diseases

Common Disease Types

Diseases are caused by fungi, bacteria and viruses — each behaves differently and needs a different management approach.

Fungal Diseases

Examples: Blasts (rice), rusts (wheat), blights (potato/tomato), mildews, leaf spots, wilts.

Recognition: Spots with defined margins, powdery growth, rust pustules, rapid leaf death.

General management: Resistant varieties, clean seed, crop spacing for airflow, rotation, and timely labelled fungicide where advised.

Bacterial Diseases

Examples: Bacterial leaf blight (rice), bacterial wilt (tomato, brinjal), citrus canker.

Recognition: Water-soaked lesions, leaf streak yellowing, sudden wilting while leaves stay green.

General management: Certified disease-free seed/planting material, field sanitation, avoiding overhead irrigation, resistant varieties.

Viral Diseases

Examples: Leaf curl (cotton, chilli, tomato), yellow mosaic (pulses, soybean), tungro (rice).

Recognition: Mosaic mottling, curling and puckering of leaves, stunted bushy plants.

General management: Control the insect vectors (whitefly, aphids), remove and destroy infected plants early, use resistant varieties — sprays do not cure viruses.

The Smart Approach

Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

IPM combines multiple safe methods and uses chemical control only as a last resort and exactly as directed. It protects crops, people, beneficial insects and the environment.

A

Crop Monitoring

Walk the field weekly (twice weekly for vegetables) and inspect leaves, stems and fruits for early signs.

Key Practices

  • Scout in a W-pattern and note pest counts
  • Use pheromone and sticky traps to detect build-up early
  • Record weather — humidity and rain trigger many outbreaks
B

Biological Control

Protect and encourage the natural enemies that eat pests — ladybirds, lacewings, spiders, parasitoid wasps and birds.

Key Practices

  • Avoid broad-spectrum sprays that kill beneficial insects
  • Release bio-agents such as Trichogramma where locally available
  • Use microbial products (e.g., Bt, NPV) as per label and local advice
C

Cultural Practices

Farming practices that make the field less friendly to pests and diseases before they start.

Key Practices

  • Resistant varieties and crop rotation
  • Timely sowing to escape peak pest periods
  • Balanced fertilizer (excess nitrogen attracts sucking pests)
  • Destruction of crop residue and infested plants; deep summer ploughing
D

Mechanical Control

Physical methods that remove or block pests directly.

Key Practices

  • Hand-picking caterpillars, clipping infested shoots
  • Yellow/blue sticky traps for whitefly and thrips
  • Light traps, bird perches, netting in nurseries and pheromone traps

Safe & Responsible Pesticide Use (Last Resort)

When pest levels cross the economic threshold and other methods are insufficient, a labelled pesticide may be advised. Follow these safety principles at all times:

  • Use only products registered for your crop and pest, in the dose on the label — never mix products on your own.
  • Follow the product label and guidance from your local agricultural department or KVK for any application decision.
  • Wear protective clothing (mask, gloves, long sleeves); never spray against the wind.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) printed on the label before harvesting.
  • Store products locked away from children, food and animals; dispose of empty containers as directed — never reuse them.
  • Keep pesticides away from water bodies, and wash hands thoroughly after handling.

Educational noteThis page does not provide application recipes or chemical mixing instructions. Product choice, dose and timing must always come from the product label and local agricultural guidance.
