Cultivation Approaches

Farming Methods

From age-old traditional practices to satellite-guided precision farming — understand what each method means, its features, advantages, limitations and where it works best.

01

Traditional Farming

Farming based on indigenous knowledge passed through generations — bullock power, farm-saved seed, local varieties and natural manures.

Main Features

  • Low external input cost
  • Local crop varieties and mixed cropping
  • Farmyard manure and natural fertility cycles

Advantages

  • Low cost and low risk
  • Environment-friendly
  • Preserves local biodiversity and knowledge

Limitations

  • Lower yields per acre
  • Dependence on rainfall and manual labour

Suitable Applications

  • Small and marginal farms, subsistence agriculture, rainfed areas
02

Modern Farming

Mechanised, input-intensive agriculture using improved seed varieties, fertilizers, plant protection and machinery to raise productivity.

Main Features

  • High-yielding varieties and hybrids
  • Tractors, harvesters and machinery
  • Assured irrigation and agri-inputs

Advantages

  • High productivity and scale
  • Time and labour saving
  • Supports food security

Limitations

  • Higher cost of cultivation
  • Risk of soil degradation and over-use of chemicals if unmanaged

Suitable Applications

  • Commercial grain production, irrigated belts, market-oriented farms
03

Organic Farming

A production system that avoids synthetic fertilizers and pesticides, relying on compost, green manure, rotation and biological pest control.

Main Features

  • No synthetic chemical inputs
  • Compost, FYM, bio-fertilizers
  • Certification systems (e.g., NPOP) for markets

Advantages

  • Improves soil health and biodiversity
  • Premium prices in organic markets
  • Safe residues, healthy food

Limitations

  • Transition period with possible yield dip
  • Certification and marketing require effort

Suitable Applications

  • Vegetables, fruits, spices, export-oriented and health-conscious markets
04

Natural Farming

A chemical-free approach (promoted in India as natural farming) built on desi cow-based preparations like Jeevamrut, mulching and diverse cropping.

Main Features

  • Cow-based microbial preparations
  • Heavy mulching and zero/minimal tillage
  • Multi-species cropping

Advantages

  • Very low input cost
  • Builds soil life and moisture retention
  • Suits resource-poor farmers

Limitations

  • Requires local livestock and skill building
  • Results vary widely with practice quality

Suitable Applications

  • Smallholder vegetable and field crops, kitchen gardens
05

Sustainable Farming

Farming that balances productivity, profitability, environment and social wellbeing so that resources remain healthy for future generations.

Main Features

  • Soil and water conservation
  • Integrated nutrient and pest management
  • Efficient energy and input use

Advantages

  • Long-term productivity with lower risk
  • Climate resilience
  • Protects farm ecology

Limitations

  • Needs planning, records and patience
  • Benefits build over seasons, not instantly

Suitable Applications

  • All farm types; ideal long-term strategy everywhere
06

Precision Farming

Managing fields at a fine scale using GPS, maps and sensors — applying the right input at the right place, in the right amount, at the right time.

Main Features

  • GPS-guided machinery and variable-rate application
  • Soil and crop sensing
  • Field-level data records

Advantages

  • Saves seed, fertilizer, pesticide and water
  • Higher efficiency and uniform quality

Limitations

  • High equipment cost
  • Needs technical skills and connectivity

Suitable Applications

  • Large and progressive farms, horticulture blocks, contract farming
07

Smart Farming

Farming powered by connected digital technology — IoT sensors, drones, mobile apps, AI and satellite imagery for monitoring and decisions.

Main Features

  • IoT soil and weather sensors
  • Drone scouting and spraying
  • App-based advisory and automation

Advantages

  • Real-time decisions and early warnings
  • Reduces waste and guesswork

Limitations

  • Technology cost and internet dependence
  • Digital skills required

Suitable Applications

  • High-value crops, protected cultivation, tech-forward FPOs
08

Mixed Farming

Combining crop production with livestock, poultry, fishery or agroforestry on the same farm so each enterprise supports the other.

Main Features

  • Crop + animal integration
  • Manure recycles nutrients to fields
  • Multiple income streams

Advantages

  • Income throughout the year
  • Risk spread across enterprises
  • Better resource recycling

Limitations

  • More management effort and skills
  • Needs housing and feed management

Suitable Applications

  • Family farms, dairy + fodder systems, integrated farming systems
09

Crop Rotation

Growing different crops in sequence on the same field — for example cereal followed by legume — instead of the same crop every season.

Main Features

  • Planned seasonal sequences
  • Legumes restore nitrogen naturally
  • Breaks pest and disease cycles

Advantages

  • Improves and maintains soil fertility
  • Reduces pest, disease and weed build-up
  • Balances nutrient demand

Limitations

  • Requires planning and market for diverse crops
  • Short-term income may differ from monoculture

Suitable Applications

  • Cereal–pulse rotations, vegetable systems, rice–wheat belts
10

Protected Cultivation

Growing crops under structures — greenhouse, polyhouse, shade-net or tunnels — that control temperature, humidity, light and pests.

Main Features

  • Controlled climate and irrigation (often drip + fertigation)
  • Off-season, year-round production
  • High-value hybrids and quality produce

Advantages

  • Very high yield and quality per area
  • Protection from extreme weather
  • Premium off-season prices

Limitations

  • High setup and maintenance cost
  • Requires trained management

Suitable Applications

  • Capsicum, tomato, cucumber, flowers, nursery raising
